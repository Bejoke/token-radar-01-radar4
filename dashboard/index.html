How the model works

One function scores every protocol. Nothing is hard-coded by name: the same five multipliers price every row, and the category multiples and sector momentum are computed from this snapshot's own live-token protocols.

  1. Category multiple — the median market-cap-over-TVL of the live-token protocols in the same category, shrunk toward the global median with a prior of 3 peers: (n × category median + 3 × global median) / (n + 3), clamped to 0.05 – 1.00. Each peer's ratio is capped at 1.00 first, so one small protocol with a huge market cap cannot set its whole category.
  2. Sector momentum — 1 + 0.30 × the median 7-day TVL change of the category's live-token peers, clamped to −30% … +30%.
  3. Growth — 1 + 0.55 × tanh(7-day TVL change / 60). Smooth and bounded: a +4,448% week saturates instead of being clamped equal to a +100% week.
  4. Maturity — 0.55 + 0.45 × ln(1 + age in days) / ln(1 + 1460). A protocol that has run longer with no token is worth more, with diminishing returns; an unknown listing date counts as 180 days.
  5. Breadth — 1 + 0.05 × ln(chains), capped at 1.20.

implied value = TVL × category × sector × growth × maturity × breadth, rounded to whole dollars.

What the board leaves out

Bridges, canonical bridges, liquid staking, liquid restaking, restaked BTC and CEX protocols are out: they are wrapped or staked products of a token that already trades, not launches of their own. So are versioned pools (a name ending in V2, v2 or 2.0), names carrying the word wrapped, staked or staking, and protocols named after an exchange. The full list, with a reason for each, is on the “Not a launch” tab. The snapshot is sealed at its fetch date and never moves; the board is a reading of it, not a prediction of whether or when any token is issued.
